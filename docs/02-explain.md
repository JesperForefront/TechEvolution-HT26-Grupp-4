# Session 2 — Explain

**Tuesday 10:00–12:00 · in your Build group · paired with another team**

Keep your own repo. Your facilitator pairs you with another team. You walk
them through your app; they walk you through theirs.

## How the session runs

1. **Revisit your own repo (20 min).** Your `.ai/` files, your commit history,
   your code — with a few hours' distance. Does `.ai/domain-model.md` still
   describe what you actually built? Fix it now if not.
2. **Prepare (15 min).** Split the three topics below across the group, one
   person per topic minimum.
3. **Present (20 min: ~15 to present, ~5 for questions).** Walk the other team
   through your app. They ask, you answer.
4. **Swap roles.** The other team presents, you ask.

## What you must be able to cover

Three things, in your own words:

1. **How the app works.** A quick walkthrough — send a kudos, see it appear.
   What does a Kudos look like in this codebase, and why those fields?
2. **One decision you made, and why.** Pull it from your Decisions log or
   your "Still open" answers in `.ai/domain-model.md`. Pick one rule and ask
   yourselves: why is it that way, not just what it does.
3. **One AI suggestion you accepted, changed, or rejected — and why.**

Questions from the other team should be about your decisions, not about which
prompt produced which line. If "the AI suggested it" is your only answer for
why something works the way it does, that's worth saying out loud.

## How to give feedback

You're the audience for the other team's decisions, not their reviewer. When
something surprises you, say that it surprised you — "I expected the
validation to be in the form and it's in the store" is useful. "This is
wrong" is not, and it is usually not true.

The team presenting: hold your defence until questions are done. Explaining
your own choices out loud, to people who did not make them with you, is the
most honest feedback you will get all year.
